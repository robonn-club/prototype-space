A credible first build opens five doors, and most people go through more than one.

## Build version two

Go back to [step 1](plan.md) with a bigger claim: outdoors instead of indoors, two robots
instead of one, a task someone would pay to have done. Keep the bar from step 6. Five runs,
uncut, rebuildable.

## Turn it into a thesis or research

Bonn's groups work on exactly these problems, and a working build is the best possible
introduction: [[Autonomous Intelligent Systems]], [[Photogrammetry and Robotics]], [[PhenoRob]],
the [[Lamarr Institute]] and [[Fraunhofer IAIS]]. The [[Institute of Computer Science]] is the
route to theses and open positions.

## Enter a competition

A competition turns a first build into a team with a deadline: [[RoboCup]] and the national
qualifiers run by the [[European RoboCup Association]], the [[Field Robot Event]] for field
robots, [[RoboRacer]] for 1/10-scale racing, and the [[European Robotics League]]. Nearby,
[[b-it-bots]] at H-BRS takes students at any level of study.

## Start a company

Talk to someone before writing a business plan. It is free: [[enaCom — Uni Bonn Transfer Center]]
is the university's own startup office. Grants that take no equity come first, namely
[[Gründungsstipendium.NRW]] and the [[EXIST-Gründungsstipendium]]. Bonn's hub,
[[DIGITALHUB.DE#funding]], runs accelerator programmes, and Germany's most active early-stage
investor, [[High-Tech Gründerfonds]], is headquartered in the city.

## Before you sell anything

None of this applies to a demo; all of it applies once a product is placed on the market. Start
with [[CE marking]]. For machines, the [[Machinery Regulation (EU) 2023/1230]] applies from
20 January 2027. Anything with Wi-Fi or Bluetooth falls under the [[Radio Equipment Directive]],
and AI features under the [[EU regulatory framework for AI|AI Act]]. Check the licence of
everything you built on: [[Ultralytics YOLO]]'s AGPL, for example, requires a commercial
agreement before a product using it can be sold.
