Most robot ideas are ten projects at once. The first build is one of them: the smallest thing
that shows the idea is real. Everything else waits for version two.

## Do this

1. **Write the claim in one sentence.** *A robot that ___ in ___.* "A robot that carries a
   parcel between two desks in a seminar room" is a first build; "an autonomous logistics
   platform" is a company. If the sentence needs an *and*, cut until it doesn't.
2. **Describe the demo you will film.** One take, start to finish, no cuts. Write down what is
   in the frame, what happens, and how someone watching knows it worked. Every later step aims
   at this shot.
3. **List what it will not do.** Outdoors, in the rain, faster than walking pace, with nobody
   nearby? Rule out everything the demo does not need. That list is what keeps the project
   finishable.
4. **Pick a track.** Choose the kind of machine closest to the demo, from the table below. The
   track sets the software, the parts and the budget for every step that follows. When two
   tracks fit, take the cheaper one.
5. **Check whether it exists already.** Spend twenty minutes on [[Hackaday.io]],
   [[awesome-robotics-projects]] and GitHub. A close match is good news: start from it, and put
   your effort into the part that is new.
6. **Set a date.** Pick an event from [the list in step 6](show.md#pick-a-date), or put a date
   in your calendar using your track's time estimate. First builds without a date rarely finish.
7. **Say it out loud.** Post the sentence and the demo in the [[Robonn]] Discord, or tell one
   person who builds things. If they can describe the demo back to you, the plan is clear
   enough.

## In Bonn

- **Find people to build with.** The [[Robonn]] Discord is where club members look for
  teammates and a second opinion.
- **See what Bonn has already solved.** If the idea touches crops, maps or humanoids, a Bonn
  group has probably published on it: [[PhenoRob]], [[Photogrammetry and Robotics]] and
  [[Autonomous Intelligent Systems]] put code and datasets online.
- **Make it count for your degree.** If the build can be part of a course, project module or
  thesis, ask a supervisor now. Some university routes only open that way: the computing
  centre's free [[HRZ 3D printing service]], for one, takes student jobs only through teaching
  staff.

## Traps

- **Building a platform instead of a demo.** A general-purpose base that could do anything,
  finished next year, loses to a narrow one that does one thing next month.
- **Taking the hardest track by default.** Legs and flight add months. If the claim works on
  wheels, use wheels.
- **Keeping it secret.** Nobody steals a first build. Plenty of people will tell you the part
  they already solved.
