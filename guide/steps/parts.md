The biggest decision in this step is whether to start from a kit. For a first build the answer
is usually yes: a kit that already drives, flies or grips puts all your effort into the part
that is new.

## Do this

1. **Choose: kit, open design, or from scratch.** A *kit* is fastest. An *open design*, with
   printed parts and a published bill of materials, is cheaper and teaches more. Build *from
   scratch* only when the demo needs something no kit does.
2. **Write the bill of materials before ordering.** One row per part: quantity, price,
   supplier, link. Add spares for anything you could burn out: fuses, regulators, motor
   drivers, one extra microcontroller.
3. **Order from the right shop.** [[BerryBase]] for Raspberry Pi and maker parts,
   [[reichelt elektronik]] for general components, [[Mouser]] or [[RS]] when a part number must
   match exactly, [[Pollin]] for cheap surplus. Shops in Germany usually deliver within a few
   working days.
4. **Order boards and custom parts first, because they take longest.** Circuit boards from
   [[Aisler]] (EU) or [[JLCPCB]] (cheapest, slowest). Parts too big for a desktop printer
   through [[Craftcloud]]; laser-cut sheet parts from [[Formulor]].
5. **Print and cut what you can yourself.** At [[MakerSpace Bonn e.V.|MakerSpace Bonn]], go to
   an open evening first, then get the induction for each machine before the week you need it.
6. **Note the licence of everything you build on.** Non-commercial or AGPL terms are fine for a
   first build, but they decide what can happen later. [[Ultralytics YOLO]] is the usual
   example.

## In Bonn

- **[[MakerSpace Bonn e.V.|MakerSpace Bonn]]**: 3D printers, laser cutters, CNC and a metal
  shop, on a membership basis. Its free open evenings ("Offene Tür") are the way in, and every
  machine needs an induction before you use it alone.
- **Through your course.** For a course project or thesis, the university's
  [[HRZ 3D printing service]] prints FDM and resin parts free of charge; the job goes in through
  a member of teaching staff.
- **When Bonn lacks a machine.** [[Dingfabrik Köln]] is a train ride away on the semester
  ticket.

## Traps

- **Buying before the simulation says what you need.** The motor you like may not lift the arm
  the simulation says you will build.
- **One of everything.** The first regulator you wire is the one you burn. Buy cheap parts in
  twos.
- **Ordering from abroad when time is short.** Orders from outside the EU usually take longer
  and can bring import charges. When a deadline is close, buy in Germany.
